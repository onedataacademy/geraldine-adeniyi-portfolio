Featured case study / Healthcare

Health Link 001Hospital digital platform

Defining patient journeys and requirements for a hospital’s proposed digital front door.

Business analysisRequirements engineeringWireframesCollaborative project

Overview illustration based on the project documents; not a screenshot of a deployed system.

The business problem

Better access starts with clearer requirements.

The project brief describes patients relying on visits, calls and manual enquiries to obtain hospital information and support.

Health Link 001 proposes a responsive digital platform where patients can manage an account, find hospital information and submit enquiries. The analysis also explores a pharmacy journey, with ordering and payment responsibilities dependent on a scope decision.

The case study brings together the supplied analysis and design artefacts. It demonstrates how business needs were translated into documented requirements, testable scenarios and interface concepts.

Status: analysis and design documentation. Launch, user adoption and realised benefits are not established by these files.

The analytical approach

From the business problem
to delivery detail.

01

Understand the gap

Compare manual patient–hospital interactions with the intended digital service and identify the missing capabilities.

02

Define the boundaries

Document the MVP features, exclusions, assumptions and dependencies, including areas needing clarification.

03

Make needs testable

Structure requirements into user stories, MoSCoW priorities and Given–When–Then acceptance criteria.

04

Visualise and trace

Use wireframes to explain the proposed journeys and connect requirements to stories and design decisions.

What the documentation covers

A connected patient journey.

Four feature areas appear in the requirements and design materials. Pharmacy functionality remains subject to scope clarification.

01 / CUSTOMER ACCOUNT

Secure access and self-service

Registration, email verification, sign-in, password reset and profile management, with validation and access-control scenarios.

02 / CONTACT US

Information and enquiry handling

Contact details, location, enquiries, callbacks, routing and acknowledgement, with clear separation from emergency or clinical support.

03 / ABOUT US

Find the right hospital information

Mission, vision, services and organisational information, with content ownership and approval considerations.

04 / PHARMACY · SCOPE DEPENDENT

Explore ordering and collection

Product eligibility, prescription checks, payment where applicable and collection. The source documents differ on which capabilities belong to this team.

A closer look

Documenting uncertainty
is part of the analysis.

The requirements workbook records scope conflicts rather than treating assumptions as approved decisions.

One example is pharmacy: the scope materials both include a patient-facing ordering capability and exclude ordering, prescription handling and payments elsewhere. The workbook records the conflict, describes the implications and identifies the decision owner.

Other queries cover profile fields, notification rules, security defaults, content ownership and cross-team dependencies. These show how requirements clarification supports delivery readiness.
